Practice

How the work is made, and why.

心性, read shinshō in Buddhist usage, means original mind: the nature of mind before anything is added to it.

Shinsei began around 2011, after my Reiki master training. It is a series of sumi-e paintings and hanging scrolls, made as part of a Bodhisattva vow and rooted in meditation, Reiki, and Buddhist and Shinto practice.

Inward first

The cultivation runs inward. Reiki practice is the primary purpose, and the brush follows from it. I don’t set out to produce an effect in the viewer. If a painting brings you some quiet, or a reason to sit, that is a real consequence of the practice, but it is not the goal.

The painting is a trace

The practice and the painting are not the same thing. A painting is what remains after the practice: a residue of one moment of attention, held in ink and paper. It carries something of that moment when I am not there to offer it myself.

The other way the work travels is the direct encounter, in person, much as Reiki passes from hand to hand.

Rebirth

心性 is the one word written on the work. In English the series is introduced as Rebirth, after 新生, which sounds the same in Japanese. Other readings of the sound, such as the sacred and true nature, are left for those who want to look further.

Materials

Sumi ink on absorbent washi. Pale ink, water and mist carry most of the space. The sakura is the series’ central image.
